SPWW2 Grand Campaign — Rules

Two players. Global map, 4 turns per year (Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter). Movement is alternating and initiative-driven, with sealed orders for anything the enemy can’t see. Every battle is resolved as a head-to-head WinSPWW2 match.

Key terms

  • BG (Battle Group) — one maneuver unit on the map. Baseline 2,500 tactical points.
  • RC (Resource Center) — the sole currency. RCs pay for BGs and Support Tokens.
  • Sector — one map space. Land or sea.
  • Stack limit — 2 BGs per sector per player (exception: capitals).
  • FoW Tier — how much a BG’s owner must reveal about it (Part III, §5).

The full Glossary (Appendix B) covers every other specialized term — Contested, Lodgement, Kampfgruppe, and the rest.

PART I: GLOBAL FOUNDATIONS & ECONOMY

1. Core Framework

Scale. One Battle Group (BG) is the game’s core maneuver unit, representing a division- to corps-sized formation. Baseline strength is 2,500 tactical points, the budget used to purchase the actual units fielded when a BG’s engagement is played out as a WinSPWW2 match.

Calendar. The campaign runs in four strategic turns per year — Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter — each resolving a full sequence of play (§4).

Stacking.

  • Maximum 2 friendly BGs per sector at the end of any phase.
  • Capitals: unlimited BGs may stack, but only 2 may fight at a time (owner’s choice). Once chosen, those two are locked in for future engagements in that sector until one is destroyed, one leaves, or the attacker is destroyed or retreats.
  • Pooling. Two stacked BGs fight as one WinSPWW2 match with pooled points (2× 2,500 = 5,000).
  • Mixed stacks. Purchase restrictions (§3.1) apply separately to each BG’s half of the pool. If the stacked BGs are from different countries, see §10.5.
  • A Swap Move (§7.3) never violates the stack limit — check the destination count after the swap resolves.

Small Island Outposts. Six sectors are single atolls or islands whose real logistics footprint can’t sustain the normal 2-BG limit: Wake Island, Midway, Fiji, Samoa, Gilbert Islands, and Marshall Islands. Each is hard-capped at 1 BG per faction, full stop — this overrides even an adjacent Lodgement Rotation pool (§8.1): the extra capacity a Rotation pool grants has to sit at the other sectors in that zone, never on one of these six specifically.

2. Strategic Economy & Mobilization

2.1. Resource Centers & the Cap Audit

RCs are the sole currency. They pay for BGs and Support Tokens, and a nation’s BG count is capped by the number of secure, supplied RCs it holds.

Hard cap. A nation’s BG count is capped by the number of secure, supplied RCs it holds. Checked only during the Winter Logistics Phase (§4.4).

Which year’s ratio applies. The Winter Logistics Phase sets the cap for the coming year, not the year just finished — the Winter 1941 audit uses the 1942 column below. No audit occurs before the first Winter turn; the starting 1941 force is fixed by scenario setup.

BGs supported per RC, by year:

1941 1942 1943 1944 1945
Germany 1 1 1.5 2 2
USA 0.5 1 2 3 4
USSR 1 1.5 2 3 3

All other nations: 1 BG per RC. RCs in occupied sectors support 1 BG regardless of year — exceptions: an occupied Soviet sector previously evacuated supports only 0.5 BG for Germany (§11.1); the Caucasus is a flat-value case outside this ratio system entirely (§11.1).

Support Tokens. Purchased with CP, not collected automatically — see §2.8.

2.2. Minor-Nation RCs (Advanced)

A Minor Nation is a non-major country that fields its own BGs under a major’s faction — Romania, Bulgaria, and Hungary under Germany; Thailand under Japan; Canada, Australia, and New Zealand under UK; others as they activate (§12.2). A Minor Nation’s RCs count toward its sponsor’s RC total and BG Cap exactly like an occupied RC — flat 1 BG per RC, regardless of year.

Capacity generated by a Minor Nation’s RCs may only build that Minor Nation’s own BGs, tracked under its own name — never a sponsor-flagged major BG, and never the reverse. Minor BGs use their national experience baseline (§3.5), follow §3.1 purchase rules, and count toward the sponsor’s Infantry Minimum (§3.2) and stack limit, but are never Elite-eligible (§10.4) or given National Tokens (§10.2) unless a specific §11/§12.3 rule says otherwise.

2.3. Supply

A BG must trace a contiguous line of friendly-controlled sectors back to a controlled RC. Sectors may be contested and still trace, so long as they’re friendly-controlled.

Sea tracing. Through ocean sectors with no opposing NTF, or through contested ocean sectors between two friendly land sectors.

Weather. Storm/Blizzard blocks a sector from being crossed while tracing, and blocks that sector from serving as the RC endpoint that turn (§6.2).

Out of supply: - −20% points for each engagement, and - −20% points permanently per turn out of supply.

Floor. Supply and fatigue penalties combined can never cost a BG more than 50% of its points.

2.4. RC-to-Capital Tracing

A separate, stricter check from ordinary BG supply. Every RC must trace a contiguous friendly-controlled line back to its controller’s capital sector — the owner’s capital if it holds the RC itself, or the occupier’s capital if an enemy holds it. Recalculate at the end of any movement phase in which control or tracing could have changed.

2.5. CP Generation

Combat Points (CP) are a second, continuously-generated currency alongside the RC-to-BG-Cap relationship in §2.1 — RCs still set how many BGs a nation may field, but CP is what pays to keep those BGs at strength, buy Support Tokens and standing forces, and fund a nation’s Officer Quality. Generated every strategic turn (not just at Winter Logistics), and spendable at any point during a player’s own activation — there is no phase gate on CP purchases.

Each RC produces independently. A specific RC’s CP output is its own historically-informed value, not a flat rate applied uniformly and divided out to an average across a nation’s holdings — the Ruhr and a minor agricultural RC do not produce the same CP just because a campaign map might assign them a similar RC-point weight for BG Cap purposes. RCs without a specifically researched value fall back to a flat rate per RC-point (150 CP), pending further calibration.

Occupied RCs produce less than owned ones — half the equivalent owned rate, on top of whatever damage (§2.6) that RC has separately suffered. An occupied Soviet RC previously evacuated by Germany is halved again on top of that (§11.1), same as it already is for BG Cap purposes.

National yield. Each nation carries a standing output multiplier reflecting its wartime economic character:

Nation Yield Conditional
USA 1.4× —
UK 1.0× —
USSR 1.2× —
Germany 1.1× baseline 1.3× while the Oil War trace is active (§11.7)
Japan 1.3× +0.2× on any turn where Bomber/Fighter/Submarine purchases (§2.8) stay under 30% of that turn’s total CP spend — Army investment rewarded automatically, not by a declared stance
Italy 1.0× —
China 0.8× —

Mobilization. A nation fielding BGs at or above its RC-derived BG Cap (§2.1) is running a more total-war economy, and generates more CP for it: +30% CP at exactly the BG Cap, scaling linearly, capped at +45% for a nation at 150% of its own cap or beyond.

Damage reduces output continuously, not in fixed steps. A bombed RC’s production scales down smoothly with however much of its capacity is actually knocked out (§2.6) — there is no flat “half output while Damaged” rule. A Convoy Raid (§8.4) is different in kind: it reduces a specific RC’s output for the turn it happens only, automatically clearing the next time CP generates, rather than persisting the way bombing damage does.

2.6. RC Damage & Repair

Strategic Bombing (§8.5) no longer flips an RC through a fixed Damaged → Destroyed sequence on a flat, uniform basis. A successful strike inflicts a variable amount of CP-production damage, scaled by the size of the raid and the attacking nation’s Officer Quality (§2.7) — the same shape of formula Convoy Raiding (§8.4) already uses for submarines, so both of the game’s “attack an RC’s economic output” mechanics scale consistently with each other. A larger raid, or one launched under a more capable officer corps, does more damage; damage is capped at whatever capacity the RC has left, so a raid can never push a target past fully destroyed.

Damage accumulates and persists until repaired — unlike Convoy Raiding’s this-turn-only reduction, a bombed RC stays damaged campaign-turn over campaign-turn until CP is spent to fix it. The legacy ACTIVE / Damaged / Destroyed status label (still read by the Cap Audit, stacking rules, and other systems that predate CP) is kept roughly in sync automatically: 50% or more of an RC’s value in accumulated damage reads as Damaged, 100% as Destroyed, reverting to ACTIVE if repaired back below that threshold.

Repair costs 2 CP for every 1 CP of capacity restored. Proportional to how much is actually damaged, not a flat fee for crossing a status boundary — a lightly-damaged high-value RC is cheaper to fully repair than a heavily-damaged one, and repair can be applied partially, a little at a time, same as a BG Refit (§3.8) can be.

2.7. Officer Quality (OQ)

Each nation carries a standing Officer Quality baseline, reflecting the historical character of its officer corps — Germany and the UK start from a stronger baseline than a rapidly-expanding wartime army like the USA’s, or the USSR’s still-recovering post-1937 officer corps.

Per-BG effective OQ. A specific BG’s own effective OQ isn’t just its nation’s baseline — a veteran formation fights above it, a green one drags it down:

BG_OQ = National_OQ + (BG's experience − 60) × 0.3

This is also where OQ decline from combat losses resolves — not a separate decay mechanic, just downstream of a BG’s own experience already dropping on Shatter (§9.3) or on a heavy Refit (§3.8), which pulls that specific BG’s effective OQ down without touching the national baseline.

Two effects.

  • BG Ceiling. 2,500 × (1 + max(0, BG_OQ − 60) / 100) — a BG under a strong officer corps, or with meaningful combat experience, can be restored above the normal 2,500-point baseline. OQ at or below 60 never reduces the ceiling below baseline; only OQ above it raises things.
  • Support Token cap. floor(BG_OQ / 20) — the most tokens a single BG may have spent from inventory against it in one Combat Resolver engagement. A mixed-nation stack’s cap is the sum of each participating BG’s own individual cap, not one shared number for the whole stack. This governs only tokens spent from inventory during that engagement — it doesn’t apply to a BG’s automatic Elite Formation allocation (§10.4) or other standing national token bonuses (§12.5), which sit outside it entirely.

Growth.

National_OQ += CP_invested / OQ_INVEST_COST     (diminishing — see below)
National_OQ += 0.10 per Decisive Victory, +0.05 per Standard Victory

OQ_INVEST_COST = 200 × (National_OQ / 60) — the CP cost to raise OQ rises as OQ itself does, so a CP-rich nation can’t simply buy its way to a dominant officer corps in a handful of turns; it’s a slow, compounding institutional investment. Victory growth applies once per unique nation represented among a winning stack, never on a Stalemate.

2.8. Unified CP Purchasing

Everything below draws from the same National CP Pool, at a flat cost, available at any point during a player’s own activation:

Purchase Cost
Support Token 250 CP
Naval Task Force 750 CP
Bomber Force / Fighter Defense Force / Submarine Fleet, 1 unit 500 CP
Flak, 1 level (Germany/Japan only, max 5 per RC) 100 CP

Flak is a distinct tool from Fighter Defense Force, not a cheaper version of the same thing. Each level adds a flat +0.5 effective defense against a Strategic Bombing raid at that specific RC — cheap and individually weaker than a Fighter unit’s contribution, but never diluted the way Fighter Defense Force is (which splits across every active RC a nation holds). Cheap, concentrated, reliable local hardening for one specific RC, versus flexible-but-thin national coverage.

Carryover. Unspent CP rolls over to the next turn, capped at half of that turn’s own generation — not half of the accumulated pool. A nation that generated 3,000 CP and spent nothing carries at most 1,500 into the next turn; the rest is lost, so CP can’t simply be banked indefinitely toward one enormous single-turn purchase.

2.9. USA CP Transfer

The USA may transfer CP directly into any single Allied nation’s pool, or split it across several, up to 25% of the USA’s own CP generation for that turn in total, per turn. A direct pool-to-pool movement, with no physical token or shipment to intercept.

  • An RC that cannot trace to its controller’s capital becomes Isolated — no Support Token next Winter, and doesn’t count toward the Cap Audit RC total (§2.1) while cut off. A deliberately distinct status from Damaged/Destroyed (§8.5) — same practical effect, different cause and different recovery.
  • Isolated clears automatically, no cost, the instant the trace is reconnected. Bomb-inflicted CP damage still requires paid repair (§2.6).
  • If an RC is already Damaged or Destroyed from bombing or submarines when it also loses its capital trace, both statuses apply simultaneously — regaining the trace only clears Isolated, not the bomb or submarine damage underneath it.

3.3. Militia

MIL BGs are never built, don’t count against the BG cap, and cannot be assigned Support Tokens. They appear only via:

  • Spontaneous defense. An enemy BG enters an unoccupied sector — a static MIL BG defends that turn. If beaten, it disbands; it never retreats.
  • Home-front conversion. If an enemy BG is in a home province and the defender lacks RCs to support its BGs, excess BGs may convert to full-strength MIL instead of taking attrition. They lose all special status; experience resets to 40.
  • Sustained Chinese resistance (China only). During Partisan Activation (§4.3), if a Chinese sector’s Partisan Level is already at its cap (4) and would have increased again this turn — no Axis CAV present to block it (§3.4) — that continued pressure spawns a Chinese MIL BG in the sector, on top of the Level’s own ongoing effects. Not a one-time event: recurs every turn the condition persists, since MIL BGs don’t count against the BG Cap.

3.4. Cavalry

Cavalry BGs have the following capabilities:

  • Tactical Reconnaissance. If a CAV BG initiates or defends in a Meeting Engagement, it automatically receives 1 free REC Support Token for that engagement.
  • Anti-Partisan Operations. At the end of a strategic turn, any occupied sector tracked under §12.1 in which a Cavalry BG is present does not increase its Partisan Level.

3.7. Reconstitution

Automatic per-turn point recovery is retired — restoring a specific BG’s points is now always a deliberate, CP-funded action via Refit (§3.8), not a free flat gain every turn regardless of what happened. A BG that didn’t fight simply keeps whatever points it has; nothing heals on its own.

Reconstitution. If a BG is destroyed between Winter Audits while its faction is currently below its RC-derived BG Cap (§2.1) — the loss opened a gap that would otherwise sit empty until the next Winter Mobilization — the faction may reconstitute a BG of the same type at half strength (1,250 pts) during the very next strategic turn only. Miss that window and the opportunity is gone; waiting for the normal Winter Mobilization path (any type, full strength) is always available regardless. Deployed under the same eligibility as Mobilization (§4.4) — an Owned RC, not Damaged/Destroyed/Isolated, currently tracing supply to the capital.

3.8. BG Refit

Spends CP (§2.5) to restore a specific BG’s points directly, from that BG’s own panel — a deliberate, funded alternative to ordinary Recovery’s flat, free per-turn gain above.

Cost.

Cost = Points Requested × (1 + hops-to-nearest-owned-RC × 0.15) × National Efficiency

Distance is measured to the nearest RC the requesting nation owns — not occupied — or for the USA specifically, a Forward Staging Base (§8.6) counts as a valid anchor too, without generating CP itself. There is no hard distance cutoff; a BG far from supply simply costs more to refit, rather than being refused outright.

National efficiency, applied to Refit cost only — a property of who’s already in the field, not a blanket discount on new purchases elsewhere:

Nation Efficiency
Germany 0.7× for a BG at experience 70 or above (veteran discount)
USSR 0.5× for INF/Militia specifically; standard for all other types
Japan 0.8× for INF specifically
UK 0.8× when the route to the BG passes only through UK-controlled or uncontested sea sectors

Experience cost. The deeper the deficit being closed in a single Refit, the more it dilutes the formation’s veteran character:

Experience Loss = round((Points Restored / BG Ceiling) × 15)

A full rebuild from empty costs up to 15 experience in one go; a light top-up costs proportionally less. This makes a patient series of small Refits genuinely different from one large one — slower, but less damaging to the unit’s own experience.

Per-turn cap. No more than 1,000 points may be restored to a single BG in one turn, regardless of CP available — the guardrail against an instant full-strength recovery. A Shattered BG at 0 points needs a minimum of three separate Refit actions, across separate turns, to reach full strength even with an unlimited CP pool.

Activation. Refit spends that BG’s activation for the turn, exactly like moving it — a BG cannot both move and Refit in the same turn.

Fog of War. Logged Sealed by default (§5.2) — an enemy shouldn’t see a Refit coming for free. Like any other Sealed, BG-tied action, it can still be exposed by B-Dienst Intercept (§5.3) if conditions for that happen to trigger.

3.9. Mobilization

Building a new BG is available any strategic turn, not just following a Winter Audit — a nation checks live against its current RC-derived BG Cap (§2.1) every time, so capturing territory mid-year opens mobilization room immediately rather than waiting for the next Winter to catch up. Not automatic: a nation under its cap doesn’t get a BG for free, it gets the option to raise one.

Where. From the Battle Group Roster section of that faction’s dashboard. Owner picks name, type (respecting §3.2’s Infantry Minimum), and deployment sector — any Owned RC, not Occupied, that is not Damaged or Destroyed and currently traces supply to the capital (§2.4). The Home Capital itself always qualifies.

Strength. A new BG need not be raised at full strength — owner selects anywhere from 50% to 100% of that BG’s OQ-adjusted ceiling (§2.7), not a flat 2,500 points. A lower-strength formation costs less to raise, but starts with less to lose before it needs its first Refit.

Cost. 1 CP per point raised, same base rate as Refit (§3.8) — no distance friction (mobilization already requires an in-supply RC) and no national efficiency discount (a brand-new formation has no veteran cadre to discount for).

Cost = Points Raised × 1

Eligibility is rechecked at the moment of purchase, not just when the dashboard was opened — if RC holdings changed since then, a nation that’s since reached its cap is correctly refused rather than mobilizing past it on stale information.

PART II: SEQUENCE OF PLAY

4. The Strategic Turn

Each strategic turn resolves the following phases in order:

4.1. Strategic Bombing

Declared before Movement. The full procedure — basing requirements, standing-force resolution, variable CP damage, and repair — is at §8.5 and §2.6.

4.2. Alternating Movement & Naval Assignment

Players take turns activating one BG at a time until both pass in succession. NTFs may be assigned to sea sectors at any point during a player’s own Alternating Movement, up to that year’s allotment — there is no separate upfront Naval Assignment step. The full movement sequence, activation types, and engagement triggers are at §7; NTF assignment rules are at §8.3.

4.3. Partisan Activation

In any Axis-occupied sector tracked under §12.1, increase Partisan Level by +1, subject to that region’s cap. Full spawn conditions, level effects, and engagement mechanics are at §12.1.

4.4. Winter Logistics Phase

Winter turn only. In order:

  • Audit. Count secure, supplied RCs, and apply the ratio for the coming year (§2.1). Soviet evacuated industry is inactive for this step (§11.4).
  • Attrition. Each BG over the newly-set cap loses 20% of its points, applied in priority order: ARM → MECH → AIRBORNE → MARINE → INF.
  • Refit. Any Shattered BG that spent the Winter turn in a friendly RC sector returns to Ready.
  • Doctrine activation. A nation may declare a Doctrine now — see §10.3.

Mobilization, Support Token purchase, and RC repair are not Winter-only steps — see §3.1 (Mobilization), §2.8 (Tokens), and §2.6 (RC Repair). All three are CP-funded and available any strategic turn a nation is eligible, not gated to this phase.

Soviet evacuated RCs reactivate after all purchases in this phase are complete (§11.4).

PART III: OPERATIONAL MECHANICS & COMBAT

5. Reconnaissance & Fog of War

Every BG has an FoW Tier, recalculated at the start of each turn, that governs what the opponent is told about it.

5.1. FoW Tiers

Tier Name What the opponent learns How a BG qualifies
1 No Info Nothing. The BG’s existence is not revealed. Friendly-controlled sector, not adjacent to any enemy-controlled/contested sector or enemy-held sea zone, and not the target of enemy REC/INL/ULTRA this turn.
2 Partial Info Sector location and BG category (ARM/MECH/INF/A-M/MIL) only — not points, experience, fatigue, or tokens. Fails the Tier 1 test: borders enemy territory or contested/enemy-controlled sea.
3 Full Info Everything — exact points, experience, fatigue, assigned tokens. Currently in or just finished combat this turn; sits in a Contested sector; or is the direct target of an enemy REC, INL, or ULTRA effect.

A BG’s tier can change every turn as the map shifts. Downgrading from 3 to 1 requires a full turn back in secure friendly territory with no enemy contact.

5.2. Sealed Orders

Sealed moves activate in the same alternating sequence as any other activation (§7.2). Only what’s shown to the opponent differs.

A BG qualifies for a Sealed activation if both its origin and destination sector are Tier 1 before and after the move. On a player’s turn, they may:

  • Announce a visible move (states which BG, from where, to where), or
  • Seal a qualifying move (announces only “a BG is activating”), or
  • Pass.

Either way it uses up the player’s activation and passes initiative under the normal rules (§7.2).

Recording a Sealed move. Each player privately records their own Sealed moves — turn, BG, origin, destination — the instant they’re made, kept private from the opponent. The instant a Sealed BG stops qualifying for Tier 1 — it becomes adjacent to the enemy, is targeted by REC/INL/ULTRA, or fights — that record becomes shared and is marked Revealed.

Any move that starts or ends in Tier 2/3 territory is not eligible for sealing — it must be announced.

5.3. B-Dienst Intercept

A rare, automatic, per-turn chance that a low-experience Allied BG’s poor communications security leaks a partial reveal to the Axis — the reverse of the Allied intelligence advantage in §10.2, but occasional rather than a standing, repeatable tool, and with no equivalent for Japan.

  • Automatic, once per strategic turn. Every currently Tier 1 Allied BG gets its own independent chance, computed as (100 − experience) / 10000 — a Militia-experience BG (40) is roughly 0.6% likely per turn, an Elite-experience BG (80+) roughly 0.2% or less. If more than one BG rolls a success in the same turn, exactly one is chosen at random — never more than a single intercept per turn.
  • Always partial. The reveal is either the BG’s approximate location and category (the Tier 2 standard, §5.1 — never exact points, experience, or fatigue), or, if that BG made a Sealed move this turn, exactly one of four fragments about it: the unit’s identity, its origin, its destination, or the type of move — never more than one at once. Which of the two overall categories is shown depends on what’s available that turn; if the BG has no Sealed move logged, it’s always the location-and-category version.
  • No player action required and no token cost. This isn’t a purchase or a standing ability — it simply may or may not happen automatically as part of advancing the turn.

6. Environmental Effects & Climate

6.1. Terrain Types

Every land sector has a major terrain type: Plains, Mountain, Coastal, Steppe, Arctic/Tundra, Forest, Desert, or Jungle. This determines which of the tactical resolution’s five terrain categories applies when setting up the corresponding WinSPWW2 match: Open/Steppe, Urban, Mountain/Jungle, Amphibious Landing, Arctic/Tundra.

Choosing the tactical category:

  • If the sector holds an active RC, use Urban — dense city fighting dominates the tactical picture regardless of what’s around it.
  • Otherwise, map the major terrain type across: Plains/Steppe/Desert → Open/Steppe; Mountain/Forest/Jungle → Mountain/Jungle; Arctic/Tundra → Arctic/Tundra directly.
  • Coastal sectors normally fall under Open/Steppe too, except during the Beach Assault phase of an Amphibious Invasion (§8.1) or a Strategic Sealift landing under fire — use Amphibious Landing there instead.

The tactical terrain category multiplies each BG’s effective points by type — ARM gets +15% in Open/Steppe but −20% in Mountain/Jungle, INF stays flat everywhere, and Airborne/Marine formations favor Amphibious Landing. This is a judgment call made fresh for each engagement: two different battles in the same sector (an open-field Meeting Engagement versus a beach landing) can legitimately use different categories.

6.2. Weather

Every sector rolls one of five conditions each strategic turn: Clear, Light Cloud, Overcast/Rain, Heavy Rain/Snow, Storm/Blizzard.

  • Weighted by season and region, not uniform. Summer skews toward Clear; Winter skews toward the two worst conditions. Arid regions (North Africa, Middle East) skew Clear regardless of season. Tropical regions (Africa, Asia, Pacific) run wetter year-round. Cold regions (USSR, Eastern Europe) skew hardest toward Storm/Blizzard specifically in Winter.
  • Influenced by its own recent history and its neighbors’ — never determined by either. A sector coming out of severe weather is more likely to roll severe weather again next turn than a sector coming out of Clear, and a sector surrounded by bad-weather neighbors leans the same way — but every condition stays reachable from every other one, with no lockouts.
  • Heavy Rain/Snow or Storm/Blizzard blocks Strategic Movement (§7.4) at the origin sector — ordinary Tactical Movement (1 sector) is unaffected.
  • Overcast/Rain, Heavy Rain/Snow, or Storm/Blizzard blocks Airborne Invasion (§8.2) at the origin sector — a lower bar than Strategic Movement’s, since accurate parachute and glider drops need real visibility and calm air in a way ground movement through the same rain doesn’t.
  • Heavy Rain/Snow or Storm/Blizzard applies −20% tactical budget to the attacker, in any sector, regardless of nation or region. Stacks with Rasputitsa (§6.4) where both apply.
  • Storm/Blizzard cuts a supply trace (§2.3) — a sector in Storm/Blizzard can’t be crossed while tracing to an active RC, and can’t itself serve as the RC endpoint that turn. Doesn’t affect a BG’s own sector if it’s already sitting there; this only blocks extending the trace into a new sector.
  • Heavy Rain/Snow or Storm/Blizzard grounds AIR, STRAT, and Fighter tokens — real air support doesn’t fly in conditions that would also block Strategic Movement.
  • Governs the visibility conditions used to set up the corresponding WinSPWW2 match — each condition maps to its own visibility range, anchored to the principle that low visibility makes air support unlikely.

6.3. River Crossings

Ten sector connections cross a named river: the Rhine (Amsterdam–Ruhr), Elbe (Bavaria–Prague), Meuse (Paris–Brussels), Volga (Stalingrad–Rostov), Dnieper (Kiev–Rostov), Oder (Berlin–Warsaw), Yangtze (Shanghai–Nanking), Irrawaddy (Burma–India (Calcutta)), Volturno (Rome–Naples), and Danube (Vienna–Budapest).

When an attack crosses one of these connections into an uncontested sector (no friendly BG of the attacker’s already there), the river must be present in the corresponding WinSPWW2 engagement map. If the destination sector is already contested by a friendly BG — the attacker is reinforcing an existing fight rather than making a fresh crossing — no river is present in that engagement; the crossing already happened when the first BG got there.

An Airborne Lodgement (§8.2) waives a river crossing entirely for its one qualifying following engagement, regardless of which named connection it crosses.

6.4. Rasputitsa (Mud Season)

Any sector in the Eastern Europe region rolling Overcast/Rain or worse (§6.2): max 1 sector move, no Strategic Movement, and −10% tactical budget if attacking. Stacks with §6.2’s own severe-weather attack penalty rather than replacing it — mud bogging down vehicles and a general storm disrupting the attack are different problems, both plausibly present on the same bad day.

6.5. Pacific Typhoon Season

Any Amphibious Invasion (§8.1) landing in a Pacific-region sector rolling Storm/Blizzard (§6.2) suffers Interdiction (−250 points, §8.3) as if contested by 1 enemy NTF, even if no enemy NTF is present.

6.6. General Winter

Winter 1941 only: German BGs in Soviet sectors attack at 50% points and defend at 80% points — a guaranteed floor, independent of that turn’s actual weather. If the sector’s rolled Weather (§6.2) is Storm/Blizzard that turn, an additional penalty stacks on top: attack drops to 40%, defend to 72%. Motti Tactics (§12.3) exempts Finland from both the floor and the additional penalty.

6.7. Desert Outflanking

An INF or MIL BG defending in a North Africa or Middle East sector against an attacking ARM or MECH BG cannot simply hold on a Stalemate result: the defender is forced to retreat 1 land sector, with no extra retreat penalties or XP losses.

Exceptions (the defender holds normally):

  • The sector is a defined bottleneck (El Alamein).
  • The sector’s major terrain (§6.1) is Mountain.
  • The sector is a coastal RC sector under the defender’s own control (a fortified port).
  • An ANZAC Battle Group is present among the defenders — the Diggers rule (§12.3) exempts ANZAC from Forced Retrograde entirely, on top of its existing Fatigue immunity.

7. Strategic & Tactical Movement

7.1. Strategic Initiative

Initiative — who takes the first action in Alternating Movement — alternates by turn: Axis first on odd-numbered strategic turns, Allies first on even-numbered turns.

7.2. Alternating Movement

BGs move one at a time, in a single unified sequence:

  • The player with initiative either activates one BG — an announced move, a sealed move (§5.2), a Swap Move (§7.3), a Strategic Movement (§7.4), a Strategic Sealift (§7.5), or a special-movement trigger like an Airborne Invasion or Amphibious Invasion (§8.1–8.2) — or passes.
  • If the activation causes the BG to enter a sector containing an enemy BG, resolve the Engagement Check (§9.1) and Tactical Resolution immediately.
  • If a battle occurred: the victor chooses whether to activate again or pass. A Stalemate (§9.3) counts as no one winning; initiative passes to the other player.
  • If no battle occurred: initiative passes to the other player automatically.
  • The turn’s movement ends when both players pass in immediate succession. Any BG that never got activated holds its position (§9.1).
  • One activation per BG per turn. A BG that has already moved, swapped, road-marched, or sea-moved this turn cannot be activated again. Armor Exploitation (§9.5) is the sole exception.

7.3. Swap Move

Two adjacent friendly BGs — including a BG staged in a sea sector and a BG in the adjacent land sector — may trade places as a single activation:

  • Both BGs move simultaneously into each other’s sector.
  • Swaps are allowed in a Contested sector. Swapping does not trigger an Engagement Check. If the sector holds a live enemy BG (a Stalemate per §9.3), it stays put — the swap only changes which friendly BG occupies the space.
  • Fatigue cost. If either sector is Contested, both swapping BGs gain +1 Fatigue Point. A swap between two secure sectors costs no fatigue.
  • Check the stack limit (§1) at both destinations after the trade.
  • Uses up one activation in the Alternating Movement sequence and passes initiative normally.
  • A BG that swaps this turn cannot also Strategic Movement or Strategic Sealift this turn (§7.2).

7.4. Strategic Movement

A BG may move 2 land sectors in a single activation if both sectors it passes through/into are friendly-controlled and not Contested.

  • If the destination sector turns out to hold an enemy BG despite being expected-friendly, resolve the Engagement Check normally at the final sector only.
  • Weather can block this move entirely at the origin sector (§6.2).
  • A BG that Strategic-Moves this turn cannot also Swap or Strategic Sealift this turn (§7.2).

7.5. Strategic Sealift

A BG in a coastal land sector may, as its single activation, move directly to any other coastal land sector connected by an unbroken chain of sea sectors, provided:

  • No sea sector along the route is under enemy Sea Control (§8.3).
  • Naval Superiority penalties still apply. If any leg is contested and the enemy holds Naval Superiority there, the moving BG suffers the normal Interdiction penalty — 250 points per NTF of the enemy’s margin (§8.3).
  • This is for repositioning into empty or friendly coastal sectors. Landing on enemy-occupied territory is an Amphibious Invasion instead (§8.1).
  • A BG that uses Strategic Sealift this turn cannot also Swap or Strategic Movement this turn (§7.2).

7.6. Tactical Sealift

Strategic Sealift is deliberately RC-to-RC with no hop limit — long-range power projection. A small, RC-less outpost that isn’t directly adjacent to a friendly BG has no valid way to receive one under that rule — it can’t be a Strategic Sealift destination (no RC), and it can’t be an Amphibious Invasion target either (that’s for taking enemy territory, not reinforcing friendly territory). Tactical Sealift is the short-range counterpart that fills that gap:

  • Range: 2 sea sectors, matching NTF Deployment Range’s own scale — not unlimited like Strategic Sealift.
  • Destination must be friendly-controlled or unoccupied and not Contested — the same “empty or friendly” standard as Strategic Sealift.
  • No RC requirement on either end. This exists specifically for the small outposts Strategic Sealift structurally can’t reach.
  • Still blocked by enemy Sea Control along the route, the same as Strategic Sealift.

7.7. Coordinated Attack

When a player’s movement into an enemy sector triggers an engagement with an opposing BG (not a spawned MIL BG), the attacking player may activate a second BG not previously activated in any adjacent sector to also enter the space and join the engagement. This counts as the second BG’s activation.

7.8. Mobile Reserve

When an attacking player’s movement into an enemy sector triggers an engagement, and the defending player has only 1 BG in the sector, the defending player may activate an adjacent ARM or MECH BG not previously activated to join the engagement. This counts as the second BG’s activation. The engagement automatically becomes a Meeting Engagement.

8. Combined Arms Operations

8.1. Amphibious Invasion

An Amphibious Invasion is triggered by moving a Battle Group from a sea sector into an enemy-occupied land sector.

Staging and operational limits.

  • Origin. The invading BG must stage from a friendly-controlled land or sea sector. An invasion cannot be launched out of contested or enemy-controlled territory.
  • Range. The target enemy-occupied land sector must be within 2 sea sectors of the staging sector, counting the unbroken sea-sector chain between them.
  • Intelligence status. A BG staged in a sea sector loaded for an invasion is always exposed at a minimum of Tier 2, Partial Info (§5.1) — the attacker cannot hide that an invasion fleet is active, only its exact composition.

Coordinated Airborne Vanguard. An amphibious landing can be executed as a joint-force operation by coordinating with an Airborne Invasion (§8.2) launched into the exact same target sector during the same strategic turn.

  • The Airborne phase is resolved first.
  • If the Airborne BG wins, an Airborne Lodgement is established, and the subsequent Beach Assault receives that Lodgement’s benefits (§8.2).
  • If the Airborne BG loses, it is Annihilated. The lodgement fails, the beach assault is cancelled, and no further BGs may enter the sector.

Resolution phases. If no Airborne Vanguard is used, or the Vanguard succeeds, the invasion resolves as two sequential tactical battles:

  • Phase 1: Beach Assault — Assault/Defend parameters (§9.2). Defeat: the invading BGs retreat to their staging sea sectors and suffer an extra 50% points loss; the invasion ends. Victory: proceed to Phase 2.
  • Phase 2: Inland Breakout — Advance/Delay against the same defender’s survivors. If an Airborne Vanguard was used, the Airborne BG does not participate — it holds the rear. Defeat: the conventional forces bounce back to staging; an un-relieved Airborne BG is left surrounded and permanently destroyed at the end of the movement phase. Stalemate: both sides remain, the sector becomes/remains Contested; Follow-On Exploitation is blocked, no out-of-sequence reinforcements may land, and relieving these forces requires a Swap Move (§7.3) — an Airborne BG present survives but stays stuck in the pocket. Victory: the defender retreats, the sector is secured, and an Amphibious Lodgement is established — the sector’s stacking limit is temporarily raised to 4 BGs until the end of the current strategic turn.

Follow-On Exploitation. On a Decisive or Standard victory in Phase 2, the attacker may immediately exploit with fresh follow-on waves: up to 2 BGs that haven’t activated yet this turn, are in a connected coastal or secure sea staging sector, and aren’t subject to naval interdiction on their route (no enemy Naval Superiority or Sea Control), may execute an out-of-sequence Strategic Sealift directly into the secured sector. This counts as each BG’s activation for the turn, and fills the temporary 4-BG cap.

Lodgement Contraction. If Follow-On Exploitation was used to stack up to 4 BGs, the player must reduce the sector back to the standard 2-BG limit during the very next strategic turn’s movement phase, by moving the excess out to adjacent friendly or empty sectors. Failing to do so by the end of that turn’s movement phase subjects the excess BGs to immediate, permanent over-stacking attrition — 20% of their points lost per turn.

Pacific island connections. A small number of Pacific sectors are directly connected for movement despite being separated by open ocean — New Guinea–Solomon Islands, Philippines–Mindanao–Dutch East Indies, Marshall–Caroline–Gilbert Islands, and Hawaii to the US West Coast or Mexico. Moving into an enemy-occupied sector across one of these connections always resolves under this section, never a plain Engagement Check. This does not apply to contiguous mainland (Australia’s Sydney–Darwin–Perth) or to movement between the Japanese Home Islands, which follow their own rules (§11.2).

Post-Lodgement Stacking. A successful Amphibious Lodgement reaches the full 4-BG stacking cap for free, automatically, for the strategic turn of the victory only — this is unconditional. Sustaining elevated capacity past that turn requires one of two independent paths:

  • Lodgement Rotation. The Allied player may spend 1× AMPH token every turn — no RC capture required — to maintain Lodgement Rotation at a secured invasion sector. While active and paid up for the current turn, that sector and every sector directly adjacent to it together support up to 6 BGs total — a single shared pool across the whole zone, not 6 in each sector individually. As with a Home Capital (§1), only 2 BGs ever fight at a time in a given engagement (the player’s choice when resolving it). The AMPH token must be spent again on every subsequent turn to keep it running; skipping a turn’s payment immediately reverts the cap to 2 (or to whatever a nearby RC provides, per Deep-Water Port Capture below) and normal Lodgement Contraction rules apply to any excess.
  • Deep-Water Port Capture. Separately, and requiring no AMPH token, the Allied player may instead physically capture and secure an adjacent land sector containing a Resource Center. The captured RC operates normally — economy, mobilization eligibility, and so on — with no stacking bonus of its own. It’s a legitimate long-term alternative to paying for Rotation, just not one that grants extra stacking capacity by itself.

8.2. Airborne Invasion

Triggered when an Airborne BG using an ABN token moves into an enemy-occupied sector. Range: 1 or 2 sectors. Blocked by Overcast/Rain or worse at the origin sector (§6.2).

Resolved as a single engagement — Airborne Insertion, Meeting Engagement parameters (§9.2), attacker may purchase airborne units only. Loss: the BG is destroyed. Win: the sector is secured, and an Airborne Lodgement is established there.

Lodgement Benefits. The next immediate combat in that sector — whether an ordinary Coordinated Attack (§7.7) from other forces or a paired Amphibious Invasion’s Beach Assault (§8.1) — receives all three of the following, regardless of which triggers it:

  1. Artillery Cutoff — the defender cannot assign ART tokens to that engagement.
  2. Blocked Reserves — the defender cannot activate a Mobile Reserve (§7.8) for that engagement.
  3. River Crossing Waived — if the attack would otherwise have required a river crossing (§6.3), it no longer does, regardless of which named connection it crosses.

Fortifications are unaffected — the defender keeps their full Assault/Defend fortifications budget. These benefits apply to exactly one following engagement, whichever happens first; they don’t persist beyond it.

This is the entire Airborne Invasion regardless of what happens next — the same one engagement, the same two outcomes, whether it’s launched standalone, paired with an Amphibious Invasion as a Vanguard (§8.1), or followed up afterward by an ordinary Coordinated Attack (§7.7) from other forces.

8.4. Submarine Warfare

Not token-driven — a standing Submarine Fleet, tracked per nation (Germany, USA only; no other nation’s WWII submarine campaign was significant enough to warrant one), separate from NTFs. Reflects real history: Germany’s U-boat arm remained substantial even as its surface navy shrank to almost nothing after 1941.

Starting strength. The 1941 column is each fleet’s starting size at scenario start. Growth beyond that is purchased with CP (§2.8), 500 CP per submarine, available any time during a player’s own activation — no longer an automatic Winter addition:

1941 starting strength
Germany 3
USA 1

Deployment is an activation, spent in place of moving a BG during Alternating Movement (§7.2) — not a token spend. A player may commit any portion of their current fleet (partial commitment, no requirement to go all-in on one activation); the committed portion leaves the standing fleet entirely for the rest of that turn, freeing it up again only once the patrol resolves. Range: 4 sectors from the deploying nation’s home capital, any sector type counted as a hop — tunable, no strong historical anchor for the exact figure, proposed as a starting point.

Effect, chosen at deployment:

  • Interdiction — as Naval Superiority (§8.3) above, applied at that sea sector.
  • Convoy Raiding — reduces one adjacent enemy-or-Neutral RC’s CP output (§ CP Economy) for this turn only; no RC status change, no Damaged/Destroyed. If more than one qualifying RC is adjacent, one is selected at random. Reduction = min(that RC’s own CP output for the turn, 50 × submarines committed × the patrolling nation’s Officer Quality ÷ 60) — a stronger, better-led patrol strips more, but can never push the target below zero for the turn. If the target RC is Neutral, half the reduction is instead credited to whichever of UK or USA has fewer hops from its own home capital to the target — a Neutral being raided pushes its sympathies toward the Allies rather than the CP simply vanishing. No such redirect when the target is already an enemy RC; that CP is just denied outright.

The NTF counter-attack. A deployed patrol persists at its sea sector for the rest of that strategic turn, same duration rule as an NTF assignment. If an enemy NTF is subsequently deployed to that same sector later in the same turn, it automatically ambushes the already-present patrol — anti-submarine forces hunting down a patrol whose presence is now known. Losses: 20% of the patrol base, plus 1 per NTF committed to the counter-attack, capped at the patrol’s own size. Order matters and this is deliberately asymmetric: a patrol deployed after an NTF is already sitting there doesn’t trigger this (it’s sneaking into contested waters, not being hunted) — only the reverse does.

Deploying and resolving are two separate activations, never one. A patrol can’t be deployed and then immediately resolved in the same activation — the whole point of the counter-attack window above is giving the opposing faction a real chance to respond (deploy an NTF here to intercept, or act elsewhere) before the patrol’s fate is settled. A newly-deployed patrol stays locked against resolution until the acting player has moved on to a different activation (selecting a sector, in tool terms) and come back to it.

Resolution. Whichever applies — the NTF counter-attack if an opposing NTF is present, or the patrol’s own chosen effect if not — resolves from that sea sector’s own panel. A patrol that’s never manually resolved and never ambushed still resolves automatically as the uncontested case the instant the strategic turn advances; it can never sit deployed indefinitely.

Stacking with NTF-based Interdiction. Where both a submarine patrol’s own Interdiction effect and an NTF-based Naval Superiority margin apply to the same sea sector in the same turn, they add together rather than one overriding the other — different threats to the same shipping lane compound, they don’t compete.

8.5. Strategic Bombing

Not token-driven either — a player activation, spent in place of moving a BG, using two standing forces per side: the Allied Bomber Force (USA, UK) and the Axis Fighter Defense Force (Germany, Japan).

Starting strength. The 1941 figures below are each force’s size at scenario start. Growth beyond that is no longer automatic — a Bomber Force, Fighter Defense Force, or Submarine Fleet unit is purchased with CP (§2.8), 500 CP each, same as any other CP purchase, available any time during a player’s own activation:

1941 starting strength
USA Bomber Force 1
UK Bomber Force 2
Germany Fighter Defense 2
Japan Fighter Defense 1

Basing requirements (unchanged from the target’s controlling nation):

  • German RCs — Allies must hold Southern England
  • Italian RCs — Allies must hold Tunisia
  • Japanese RCs — Allies must hold Formosa or the Marianas, or a Mobile Staging Anchorage (§8.6) within 2 sea sectors of the target RC

Range. Target RC must be within 6 sectors of the required basing sector (any sector type counts as a hop) — larger than NTF Deployment Range’s 2-sea-sector figure since aircraft cover ground surface fleets can’t; tunable, no strong historical anchor for the exact number.

RC diffusion. Germany and Japan each hold several RCs simultaneously; the Allies can mass a strike against any single one of them. Rather than the full national Fighter Defense Force defending every RC at once, each strike only ever contests against that force divided by the defending nation’s current count of active RCs — a nation holding 10 RCs with a Fighter Defense Force of 20 has, in effect, roughly 2 defending any one of them, not 20. More RCs held means thinner coverage everywhere; losing RCs concentrates what defense remains onto fewer targets.

Fighter Defense Posture, set per nation on the Axis dashboard, layered on top of RC diffusion above — a standing strategic choice, not a per-engagement one:

  • Concentrated — fewer, larger formations. Lower chance any given raid meets defenders at all, but a real punishing loss rate on the raids that do run into them.
  • Dispersed (the default) — thin coverage spread across every RC. Higher chance of contact, but each contact is a weaker formation that inflicts lighter losses.

Resolution — an odds-ratio roll, not a deterministic margin check. Compare committed Bomber Force to the target nation’s diffused, posture-adjusted Fighter Defense Force, plus a flat baseline of 3 representing ground-based flak and AA independent of the standing fighter force — applied automatically the instant a strike resolves, no waiting on the Axis player to respond, since defense is standing infrastructure (fighter bases, radar, flak), not a per-battle decision. Force size influences the outcome rather than dictating it: success chance is bombers ÷ (bombers + effective fighters + 3). The flat baseline exists specifically so RC diffusion alone can’t push success chance too high for a nation holding a lot of territory, and so a nation that’s ever driven its Fighter Defense Force to 0 doesn’t thereby guarantee every future strike — there’s always some friction from ground defenses even with no standing air force left.

  • Success: RC suffers variable CP-production damage, scaled by the raid’s size and the attacking nation’s Officer Quality — full formula and repair cost at §2.6. Bombers take a flat 10% loss. Fighters take a loss scaled by how outmatched they were at this specific engagement — a ratio of committed bombers to the diffused fighter force actually present, capped at 2× — rather than an unbounded margin against the nation’s full total. This keeps a single strike from being able to wipe out a large share of the whole Fighter Defense Force just because RC diffusion made the local defense small; losses stay proportional to what was actually there to lose.
  • Failure: no RC damage. Fighters take a flat 10% loss. Bombers take the same capped-ratio loss instead, further scaled by the defending posture’s loss multiplier (heavier under Concentrated, lighter under Dispersed).
  • The ratio still matters even when the roll goes the other way. An outnumbered raid that gets lucky and succeeds anyway still takes extra bomber losses for how outnumbered it was; a favored raid that gets unlucky and fails anyway still inflicts extra fighter losses for the fight it put up. Force size shapes both the odds and the cost of the outcome, it just doesn’t lock either one in advance, and neither side’s losses can run away unboundedly regardless of how lopsided the underlying numbers are.
  • Weather (§6.2): Heavy Rain/Snow or Storm/Blizzard at the target sector grounds the mission entirely — no launch, no losses either side.

Partial commitment, same as submarine patrols — a player may split their Bomber Force across multiple strikes in one turn (each still costing its own activation), as long as the total committed across all of that turn’s activations doesn’t exceed the force’s current total size.

Replacing losses. No longer automatic — a lost Bomber or Fighter unit is replaced the same way it was grown in the first place, by spending CP (§2.8) on a new one. A force that’s taken heavy losses simply operates smaller until CP is spent to rebuild it.

Repairing a struck RC is covered in full at §2.6 — 2 CP spent per 1 CP of damaged capacity restored, available from that RC’s own sector panel, separate from the Bomber/Fighter Force losses above (which are a property of the standing forces themselves, not the RC they struck).

8.6. NTF Deployment Range

All NTFs deployed during Movement must trace a viable line of communication back to an active RC controlled by that same nation — not merely the same faction. A UK NTF traces to a UK-controlled RC specifically; it cannot draw range from a USA, USSR, or China RC just because they’re all Allied.

  • Base Range. An NTF may only deploy to a sea sector within 2 sea sectors of a land sector controlled by that same nation and containing an active RC (or a Forward Staging Base or Mobile Staging Anchorage, below), tracing the shortest contiguous chain of sea sectors from the target back to that anchor.
  • USN Mobile Staging Anchorages. Any sea sector holding 3 or more USN NTFs acts as a virtual active RC for range-tracing purposes for other USN NTFs. A Mobile Staging Anchorage also counts as a valid friendly staging point for an Amphibious Invasion (§8.1) even without nearby coastal territory, and as valid basing for Japan’s Strategic Bombing basing requirement (§8.5) if within 2 sea sectors of the target RC.
  • Forward Staging Bases. During any Alternating Movement phase, if a US Amphibious or Marine BG occupies a coastal land or island sector with an RC value of 0, the US may spend 1× ENG or AMPH token to instantly upgrade that sector into a Forward Staging Base: it generates no economic points and supports no extra BGs, but acts as an active RC solely for tracing NTF deployment range.
  • The Island-Hopping Supply Link. When tracing the path from a target sea sector back to an active RC (or Forward Staging Base / Mobile Staging Anchorage), the path may cross at most 1 land sector. An NTF cannot bridge multiple island-to-island or island-to-mainland connections to trace supply — intermediate islands must be captured and Forward Staging Bases built to step the fleet’s reach forward.
  • Gibraltar. The sole sea-to-sea link between the Atlantic and the Western Mediterranean. As long as it is held by the standing UK garrison — a permanent fortress presence, not a Battle Group, never at risk from an engagement roll — the strait is closed to Axis navy entirely: no Axis NTF may deploy there, and no Axis NTF range tracing or amphibious route may pass through it. This is a hard block, not Interdiction. Held by the UK garrison from scenario start.
  • Suez. The sole sea-to-sea link between the Eastern Mediterranean and the Red Sea, functioning as an ordinary chokepoint sea sector — whoever holds Naval Superiority or Sea Control there gates the route in the normal way.

Activation. A Doctrine is declared during the Winter Logistics Phase (§4.4) — no Support Token cost to activate. The only cost is the structural one listed in the Doctrine’s own entry above, which starts running once it takes effect the following Spring.

Lock-in. Once active, a Doctrine cannot be switched or dropped for 1 full year (4 strategic turns).

Switching. After lock-in expires, changing to the nation’s other Doctrine is free of any token cost, same as the original activation — just that Doctrine’s own structural cost, starting fresh. 10.5. Contingent Bonus Precedence (Advanced)

If BGs from more than one country fight in the same engagement — pooled in a stack (§1), joined via Coordinated Attack or Mobile Reserve (§7.7–7.8), or reinforced by a Kampfgruppe (§11.1) — and more than one of those countries would contribute a national bonus (a National Token, §10.2, or a Minor National Rule, §12.3) to that fight, the owning player must declare one BG as that engagement’s Primary BG before Tactical Resolution.

Only the Primary BG’s country’s national bonuses apply. Every other involved BG’s national bonuses are suppressed for the fight — points, experience, standard Support Tokens, and all other rules still apply normally. The Primary BG choice is made fresh for each engagement.

Standard Support Tokens (§10.1) are never nation-locked and are unaffected. Elite Formation tokens (§10.4) are tied to each BG’s own half of the pool and apply regardless of which BG is Primary.

11.1. Germany

Kampfgruppe (KG). When determining an engagement, any German ARM or MECH BG in a sector adjacent to that engagement may detach a KG of up to 1,000 points and commit it immediately.

  • KG inherits its parent’s type and follows that type’s purchase rules.
  • One KG per engagement.
  • Does not count against the stack limit (§1).
  • Can only ever re-merge with its parent BG.

Atlantic Wall. If the Allies conduct an amphibious invasion of a Western European sector while holding no Western European sector, Germany receives 1,500 points for fortifications/bunkers and 1 free ART token.

Occupied Soviet Evacuations. A Soviet RC sector relocated under Industrial Evacuation (§11.4) and then occupied by Germany supports only 0.5 BG, not the normal 1 (§2.1).

The Caucasus. If Germany captures and holds the Caucasus sector, it counts as a flat 2 Resource Center points toward Germany’s RC total — not run through the normal year-based ratio (§2.1) or the occupied/evacuated-sector reductions above. Still subject to Partisan effects (§12.1).

SS Formations. Any German BG whose designation is prefixed “SS-” never qualifies for Home-front MIL conversion (§3.3). If over the BG Cap at Winter Attrition (§4.4), an SS-prefixed BG takes the full 20% attrition instead of converting.

11.2. Japan

Oil War. From 1943, Japan must trace supply to an active RC in Sumatra. See §11.7.

Three-Alls. Once per year, during the Movement Phase, Japan can declare a Three-Alls Campaign in a Chinese sector. In all engagements in that sector for both Spring and Summer turns, any Japanese INF or CAV BG gains 500 combat points. The sector also gains +1 Partisan Level (§12.1).

In any Chinese sector with Partisan Level below 3 and a Japanese INF or CAV BG, Japan increases the Partisan Level by 1 and restores 500 points to any Japanese INF or CAV BG on mainland China, up to its 2,500 baseline. Ketsu-Go — Operation Decision. The instant an Allied BG enters any Home Island sector (Japan (Tokyo), Japan (Kyushu), or Japan (Hokkaido)), Japan may declare Ketsu-Go. Tokkotai (Special Attack Units). A national token, capped at 1 per RC still held in the Home Islands. Usable only when defending a Home Island sector or its adjacent sea zone during an Amphibious Invasion’s Beach Assault (§8.1): destroys one enemy Support Token assigned to that invasion, or inflicts a flat −750 points to the invading BG if no token is available. Consumed on use.

Coastal Redoubt. A Japanese BG defending in a Home Island sector, Iwo Jima, or Okinawa is always treated as Assault/Defend for fortification purposes (+750 pts/BG, §9.2) regardless of how long it has held the sector. Spontaneous Defense (§3.3) in a Home Island sector also spawns two static MIL BGs instead of one.

11.3. China — War of Resistance

  • China always fields a minimum of 6 INF BGs (4 Nationalist, 2 Communist) regardless of RCs, as long as at least one Chinese-controlled sector exists (contested counts). Shortfalls made up at Winter.
  • China builds INF only, plus a maximum of one MECH BG — until the Alpha Force Trigger: if the Allied player controls both Burma and Chongqing during the 1944 or 1945 Winter Logistics Phase, this restriction lifts permanently. China may then build up to 1× ARM and 1× MECH at its national experience baseline, on top of the one-MECH allowance above.

  • The USA expends an AIR Support Token during any strategic turn

United Front. Nationalist and Communist BGs cannot stack together in the same sector, nor Swap Move with each other.

Flying Tigers. If, during the Winter Logistics Phase, Burma is controlled by the Allies and Chongqing is controlled by China, China receives one free AIR Support Token.

People’s War. China’s partisan spawn and effects are covered in §12.1.

11.4. USSR

Partisans. The USSR is the baseline case for §12.1’s partisan system.

Emergency Soviet Reserves. The first time an Axis BG enters or becomes adjacent to Moscow, Leningrad, or Stalingrad, USSR may immediately raise 2 free INF BGs at that sector, outside the normal Winter Mobilization phase and not counted against the RC-derived BG Cap (§2.1). 11.5. United Kingdom

This is always on — the UK does not need to assign an NTF there to benefit from it, though assigned NTFs still add to the total normally.

11.6. Italy

  • All remaining Italian BGs and territory become the RSI (Repubblica Sociale Italiana): its Occupier flips to Germany with no fight — RSI becomes a new Minor Nation under Germany (§2.2).

  • Elite Formations get no free token at all (down from their normal one, §10.4)
  • Only one token total may be assigned per engagement, instead of one of each type

12.5. Neutrality Violation

Most of the map’s 18 Neutral countries — Afghanistan, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Cuba, Ireland, Mexico, Mongolia, Peru, Saudi Arabia, Switzerland, Tibet, Venezuela, and the ones already covered by §12.2’s table — have no combat garrison worth mentioning. This rule attaches a cost to simply walking in and taking them.

  • Trigger. Moving a Battle Group into any Neutral-owned sector prompts a confirmation before the move commits. Declining cancels the move entirely; nothing is lost by backing out.
  • Effect, on confirmation. The invading player picks which nation of the opposing faction receives the consequence — there’s no single obvious recipient in a multi-nation alliance, so the invader names one explicitly. That nation gets a standing +0.5 RC added to its Cap Audit total, and 2 free Support Tokens of the invader’s opponent’s choice, both permanent and immediate.
  • Stacking. The +0.5 RC bonus is cumulative per nation — two separate violations awarded to the same nation total +1 RC, three total +1.5, and so on. There’s no cap.
  • This applies regardless of whether the target sector is already covered by a specific §12.2 activation trigger — the two systems aren’t mutually exclusive. Invading Iraq, for instance, still triggers its own automatic Summer 1941 activation independently of this rule.

Appendix B: Glossary of Terms

Advance/Delay — the Engagement Matrix result when attacking a BG that held last turn but not the turn before. §9.1–9.2.

Annihilation — a defeated BG with no valid retreat sector is destroyed outright. §9.5.

Assault/Defend — the Engagement Matrix result when attacking a Fortified (2+ turn stationary) BG. §9.1–9.2.

B-Dienst Intercept — a rare, automatic, per-turn chance that a low-experience Allied BG’s poor communications security leaks a partial reveal to the Axis. §5.3.

BG (Battle Group) — the game’s core maneuver unit, baseline 2,500 tactical points.

Contested — a sector where a Stalemate (§9.3) has left both sides’ BGs in place. Blocks Strategic Movement through it and several other rules that require a sector to be secure.

Elite Formation — a BG designation earned per 6 RCs (max 3/nation) granting free tokens every turn. §10.4.

Engagement Check — the test run whenever a BG’s activation would move it into a sector containing an enemy BG, determining which Match type applies. §9.1.

FoW Tier — Fog of War Tier, 1–3, governing what’s revealed about a BG to the opponent. §5.1.

Fortified — a BG that held its position for 2+ consecutive turns, triggering Assault/Defend if attacked. §9.1.

Kampfgruppe (KG) — a German detachment mechanic letting an adjacent ARM/MECH BG commit up to 1,000 points into an engagement without counting against the stack limit. §11.1.

Lodgement — the elevated stacking status (temporarily up to 4 BGs) following a successful Amphibious Invasion. §8.1.

Meeting Engagement — the Engagement Matrix result when both sides moved into the same sector on the same turn. §9.1–9.2.

NTF (Naval Task Force) — a nation’s naval strength unit, fixed allotment per year, cannot be built. §8.3.

Pan-American Solidarity — the staggered, automatic Allied activation of seven Latin American nations over the course of the war, each contributing a standing +1 RC to the USA. §12.2.

RC (Resource Center) — the sole in-game currency; pays for BGs and Support Tokens.

Sealed Order — a move announced without revealing which BG or where, available only when both origin and destination are FoW Tier 1. §5.2.

Sector — one map space, land or sea.

Shattered — the survivor status of a BG that lost a Decisive victory (§9.3); loses its next Rest cycle’s benefit and must retreat.

Stalemate — a tied or near-tied (>70%) engagement result; neither side retreats, the sector becomes/remains Contested. §9.3.

Standard Token — the common pool of Support Tokens (AIR, ART, HVY, ENG, AMPH, NAVGUN, ABN, REC, INL, STRAT, Fighter, Submarine, Lend-Lease) as opposed to a nation-specific National Token. §10.1.

Support Token — the general currency spent on a specific battle or standing effect; collected at Winter Logistics (1 per 2 RCs). §10.

Weather — one of five conditions rolled per sector each turn, influenced by season, region, and recent history; can block Strategic Movement or Airborne Invasion depending on severity. §6.2.

Appendix C: The Strategic Projection Model (SPM)

A fallback resolution framework, used only when a WinSPWW2 tactical match is terminated before completion — a connection loss, a scheduling conflict, an unresolvable technical fault — and a manual audit of surviving tactical units is not possible. Where the match completed and scored normally, use §9.3 instead; this appendix exists specifically for the case where that scoring was never generated. Its deductions are a distinct, standalone model and are not to be blended with §9.3’s outcome table.

C.1 — Applicability

Use the Strategic Projection Model only when both conditions hold:

  • The match terminated before a native win/loss/score result was produced, and
  • No manual count of surviving tactical units is available to either player.

If either condition fails — the match finished normally, or a unit count can still be reconstructed by hand — resolve the engagement through §9.3 instead. SPM is a last resort, not an alternate path chosen for convenience.

C.2 — Determining the Outcome

Outcome is derived from the single visible input available at the moment of termination: objective hex control, expressed as each side’s share of total map objective points. Compare the two shares; whichever side holds the larger share is evaluated against the brackets below to set the outcome. If the two shares are equal, or neither side holds a clear majority (below 51%), the result is always Tactical Stalemate regardless of which side is nominally larger.

Controlling side’s share of objective points Outcome
> 70% Decisive Victory
51–70% Standard Victory
50/50, or no share above 50% Tactical Stalemate

C.3 — Proportional Budget Deductions

Baseline deductions apply to each participating BG’s starting tactical budget for that match, before the Time-Elapsed Multiplier (C.4) is applied.

Decisive Victory −10% −50% Loser Shattered, must retreat (§9.4–9.5) Winner +5, Loser −5
Standard Victory −20% −30% Loser retreats normally (§9.4) Winner +3, Loser 0

C.4 — The Time-Elapsed Multiplier

A match cut short at Turn 5 of 30 has told us far less about the tactical outcome than one cut short at Turn 28. To keep the deduction proportional to how much of the battle was actually observed, the baseline figures in C.3 are never applied at full value — they are scaled by the fraction of the scenario actually played:

Applied Deduction = Baseline Outcome Deduction × (Turns Played ÷ Total Scheduled Scenario Turns)

Apply this scaling independently to the winner’s and loser’s deductions; each side’s baseline is scaled by the same fraction.

Worked Example Match called at Turn 15 of a 30-turn scenario, resolved as a Standard Victory.

Time-Elapsed fraction: 15 ÷ 30 = 0.50

Side Baseline (C.3) × Fraction Applied Deduction
Winner −20% × 0.50 −10%
Loser −30% × 0.50 −15%

C.5 — Operational Constraints & Edge Cases

  • Multi-BG stacks. If more than one BG from a side participated, calculate that side’s total Applied Deduction once, then divide it equally across every participating BG in the stack — not the full deduction to each.
  • Fatigue is never scaled. Every participating BG receives its full, mandatory +1 Fatigue Point (§3.6) for having fought, regardless of how small the Time-Elapsed Multiplier made the point deduction. A five-turn skirmish still counts as a battle fought.
  • Ammunition and consumables are not tracked separately. Their expenditure is already folded into the percentage deductions above — do not additionally deduct tokens or supply for ammunition use under SPM.
  • Support Tokens are unaffected by SPM. Token expenditure for the engagement follows the normal rules and player judgment (§10.1) — SPM only governs the BGs’ tactical point budgets.
