SPWW2 Grand Campaign — Rules

Two players. Global map, 4 turns per year (Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter). Movement is alternating and initiative-driven, with sealed orders for anything the enemy can’t see. Every battle is resolved as a WinSPWW2 PBEM custom match.

Key terms - BG (Battle Group) — one maneuver unit on the map. Baseline 2,500 tactical points. - RC (Resource Centre) — the sole currency. RCs pay for BGs and Support Tokens. - Sector — one map space. Land or sea. - Stack limit — 2 BGs per sector per player (exception: capitals). - FoW Tier — how much a BG’s owner must reveal about it. See §5.

Core vs. Advanced. Core: §1–11, base §12 (Oil War, Kampfgruppe, Atlantic Wall, Three-Alls, Rice Campaign, Home Island defense, Partisan Warfare §12.0, Italian Armistice, SOE, USSR standing rules), §13’s activation table. Advanced: §2.1 Minor-Nation RCs, §9.2.1/§9.5 Doctrines, §9.4 Contingent Bonus Precedence, §13.1/§13.2 Minor Doctrines and the French Legitimacy Track. Advanced modules may be adopted independently.

1. Sequence of Play

Each turn, in order:

  • Naval Assignment — no longer a discrete upfront step. Players may assign NTFs to sea sectors at any point during their own Alternating Movement, up to that year’s allotment (§11.4). Once assigned, an NTF’s sector is locked for the rest of the strategic turn.

  • Strategic Bombing — Allies declare bomber targets; Axis declares fighter defense; resolve.

  • Alternating Movement — players take turns activating one BG at a time until both pass in succession. See §7.

  • Partisan Activation. In any Axis-occupied sector tracked under §12.0, increase Partisan Level +1 (subject to that region’s cap).

  • Winter Logistics Phase — Winter turn only. See §10.

2. Economy: Resource Centres

Hard cap. A nation’s BG count is capped by the number of secure, supplied RCs it holds. Checked only during the Winter Logistics Phase.

Which year’s ratio applies. The Winter Logistics Phase sets the cap for the coming year, not the year just finished — e.g. the Winter 1941 audit uses the 1942 column below. No audit occurs before the first Winter turn; the starting 1941 force is fixed by scenario setup.

BGs supported per RC, by year:

1941 1942 1943 1944 1945
Germany 1 1 1.5 2 2
USA 0.5 1 2 3 4
USSR 1 1.5 2 3 3

All other nations: 1 BG per RC. RCs in occupied sectors support 1 BG regardless of year (exception: occupied Soviet sectors previously evacuated support only 0.5 BG for Germany — §12.1; the Caucasus is a flat-value case outside this ratio system — also §12.1).

Support Tokens. 1 token per 2 RCs, collected in the Winter Logistics Phase. Type chosen at collection. House rule: unspent tokens don’t carry forward past the end of the year — any token still sitting unused when a Winter Logistics Phase runs is cleared before that Winter’s fresh collection replaces it. Spend down before Winter, or lose it.

2.1. Minor-Nation RCs (Advanced)

A Minor Nation is a non-major country that fields its own BGs under a major’s faction (currently Romania, Bulgaria, and Hungary under Germany; Thailand under Japan; Canada, Australia, and New Zealand under UK — see the Economy tab mapping table, and §13 for others as they activate). A Minor Nation’s RCs count toward its sponsor’s RC total and BG Cap exactly like an occupied RC — flat 1 BG per RC, regardless of year.

Capacity generated by a Minor Nation’s RCs may only build that Minor Nation’s own BGs, tracked under its own name in the OOB — never a sponsor-flagged major BG, and never the reverse. Minor BGs use their national experience baseline (§3.5), follow §3.1 purchase rules, and count toward the sponsor’s Infantry Minimum (§3.2) and stack limit, but are never Elite-eligible (§9.3) or given National Tokens (§9.2) unless a specific §13/§13.1 rule says otherwise.

3.1.1. Terrain Types

Every land sector has a major terrain type shown on its own panel: Plains, Mountain, Coastal, Steppe, Arctic/Tundra, Forest, Desert, or Jungle. This is reference information, not itself a combat modifier — its job is to tell you which of the Combat Resolver’s five tactical terrain categories fits when you set up a WinSPWW2 match, since the Resolver uses a smaller, purpose-built set: Open/Steppe, Urban, Mountain/Jungle, Amphibious Landing, Arctic/Tundra.

Choosing the Resolver category:

  • If the sector holds an active RC (the “Urban” modifier shown alongside its major terrain), pick Urban — dense city fighting dominates the tactical picture regardless of what’s around it.
  • Otherwise, map the major terrain type across: Plains/Steppe/Desert → Open/Steppe; Mountain/Forest/Jungle → Mountain/Jungle; Arctic/Tundra → Arctic/Tundra directly.
  • Coastal sectors normally fall under Open/Steppe too, except during the Beach Assault phase of an Amphibious Invasion (§11.1.3) or a Strategic Sealift landing under fire — pick Amphibious Landing there instead.

The Resolver’s terrain choice multiplies each BG’s effective points by type — for example ARM gets +15% in Open/Steppe but −20% in Mountain/Jungle, INF stays flat everywhere, and Airborne/Marine formations favor Amphibious Landing. This is a judgment call made fresh for each engagement, not an automatic lookup — two different battles in the same sector (an open-field Meeting Engagement versus a beach landing) can legitimately use different categories.

3.3. Militia

MIL BGs are never built, don’t count against the BG cap, and cannot be assigned Support Tokens. They appear only via:

  • Spontaneous defense. An enemy BG enters an unoccupied sector → a static MIL BG defends that turn. If beaten, it disbands (it never retreats).

  • Home-front conversion. If an enemy BG is in a home province and the defender lacks RCs to support its BGs, excess BGs may convert to full-strength MIL instead of taking attrition. They lose all special status; experience resets to 40.

3.4. Cavalry

Cavalry BGs have the following capabilities:

  • Tactical Reconnaissance. If a CAV BG initiates or defends in a Meeting Engagement, it automatically receives 1 free REC Support Token for that engagement.

  • Anti-Partisan Operations. At the end of a strategic turn, any occupied sector tracked under §12.0 in which a Cavalry BG is present does not increase its Partisan Level.

3.7. Recovery

  • Not engaged this turn: +500 points.

  • Not engaged this turn and in a home province: +1,000 points.

4. Supply

A BG must trace a contiguous line of friendly-controlled sectors back to a controlled RC. Sectors may be contested and still trace, so long as they’re friendly-controlled.

Sea tracing. Through ocean sectors with no opposing NTF, or through contested ocean sectors between two friendly land sectors.

Out of supply: - −20% points for each engagement, and - −20% points permanently per turn out of supply.

Floor. Supply + fatigue combined can never cost a BG more than 50% of its points.

4.1. RC-to-capital tracing

A separate, stricter check from ordinary BG supply. Every RC must trace a contiguous friendly-controlled line back to its controller’s capital sector (the owner’s capital if it holds the RC itself, or the occupier’s capital if an enemy holds it). Recalculate at the end of any movement phase in which control or tracing could have changed.

  • An RC that cannot trace to its controller’s capital becomes Damaged — same effects as a successful strategic bombing strike (§11.3): no Support Token next Winter, and doesn’t count toward the Cap Audit RC total (§2) while cut off.

  • This kind of Damaged clears automatically, no token cost, the instant the trace is reconnected. (Bomb/sub-inflicted Damaged/Destroyed still require the normal token repair, §11.3.)

  • If an RC is already Damaged or Destroyed from bombing/subs when it also loses its trace, the worse status applies; regaining the trace only clears the isolation cause, not bomb/sub damage.

5. Fog of War

Every BG has an FoW Tier, recalculated at the start of each turn, that governs what the opponent is told about it.

5.1. The three tiers

Tier Name What the opponent learns How a BG qualifies
1 No Info Nothing. The BG’s existence is not revealed. Friendly-controlled sector, not adjacent to any enemy-controlled/contested sector or enemy-held sea zone, and not the target of enemy REC/INL/ULTRA this turn.
2 Partial Info Sector location and BG category (ARM/MECH/INF/A-M/MIL) only — not points, experience, fatigue, or tokens. Fails the Tier 1 test: borders enemy territory or contested/enemy-controlled sea.
3 Full Info Everything — exact points, experience, fatigue, assigned tokens. Currently in or just finished combat this turn; sits in a Contested sector; or is the direct target of an enemy REC, INL, or ULTRA effect.

A BG’s tier can change every turn as the map shifts. Downgrading from 3 to 1 requires a full turn back in secure friendly territory with no enemy contact.

5.2. Sealed Orders (Tier 1 movement)

Sealed moves activate in the same alternating sequence as everything else (§7.2). Only what’s shown to the opponent differs.

A BG qualifies for a Sealed activation if both its origin and destination sector are Tier 1 before and after the move. On your turn, you may:

  • Announce a visible move (says which BG, from where, to where), or

  • Seal a qualifying move (announce only “I am activating a BG”), or

  • Pass.

Either way it uses up your activation and hands over initiative under the normal rules (§7.2).

Recording and auditing a Sealed move (Google Sheet — see the companion tracker file):

  • Log it privately, immediately. Write the row (turn, BG, from, to) into your own private Sealed Log tab, never shared with your opponent.

  • Reveal on trigger. The instant a Sealed BG stops qualifying for Tier 1 — adjacent to the enemy, targeted by REC/INL/ULTRA, or fights — copy that row into the shared Public Order Board and mark it “Revealed.”

  • Dispute resolution. Open File > Version History on the private log and compare the entry’s edit timestamp to the turn/activation count it claims.

Any move that starts or ends in Tier 2/3 territory is not eligible for sealing — announce it.

6. Stacking

  • Max 2 friendly BGs per sector at the end of any phase.

  • Capitals: unlimited BGs may stack, but only 2 may fight (owner’s choice). Once chosen, those two are locked in for future engagements in that sector until one is destroyed, one leaves, or the attacker is destroyed/retreats.

  • Pooling. Two stacked BGs fight as one WinSPWW2 match with pooled points (2× 2,500 = 5,000).

  • Mixed stacks. Purchase restrictions apply separately to each BG’s half of the pool. If the stacked BGs are from different countries, see §9.4.

  • A Swap Move (§7.3) never violates the stack limit — check the destination count after the swap resolves.

6.1. Small Island Outposts (house rule)

Six sectors are genuinely tiny single atolls or islands whose real logistics footprint can’t sustain the normal 2-BG limit: Wake Island, Midway, Fiji, Samoa, Gilbert Islands, and Marshall Islands. Each is hard-capped at 1 BG per faction, full stop — this overrides even an adjacent Lodgement Rotation pool (§11.1.6): the extra capacity a Rotation pool grants has to sit at the other sectors in that zone, never squeezed onto one of these six specifically.

7. Movement

7.1. Strategic Initiative

Initiative — who takes the first action in Alternating Movement — alternates by turn: Axis first on odd-numbered strategic turns, Allies first on even-numbered turns.

7.2. Alternating Movement

BGs move one at a time, in a single unified sequence:

  • The player with initiative either activates one BG — an announced move, a sealed move (§5.2), a Swap Move (§7.3), a Strategic Movement (§7.4), a Strategic Sealift (§7.5), or a special-movement trigger like an Airborne/Amphibious Invasion — or passes.

  • If the activation causes the BG to enter a sector containing an enemy BG, resolve the Engagement Check (§7.7) and Tactical Resolution immediately.

  • If a battle occurred: the victor chooses whether to activate again or pass. A Stalemate (§8.1) counts as no one winning; initiative passes to the other player.

  • If no battle occurred: initiative passes to the other player automatically.

  • The turn’s movement ends when both players pass in immediate succession. Any BG that never got activated holds its position (§7.7).

  • One activation per BG per turn. A BG that has already moved, swapped, road-marched, or sea-moved this turn cannot be activated again. (Armor Exploitation, §8.3, is the sole exception.)

7.3. Swap Move

Two adjacent friendly BGs — including a BG staged in a sea sector and a BG in the adjacent land sector — may trade places as a single activation:

  • Both BGs move simultaneously into each other’s sector.

  • Swaps are allowed in a Contested sector. Swapping does not trigger an Engagement Check. If the sector holds a live enemy BG (a Stalemate per §8.1), it stays put — the swap only changes which friendly BG occupies the space.

  • Fatigue cost. If either sector is Contested, both swapping BGs gain +1 Fatigue Point. A swap between two secure sectors costs no fatigue.

  • Check the stack limit at both destinations after the trade.

  • Uses up one activation in the Alternating Movement sequence and passes initiative normally.

  • A BG that swaps this turn cannot also Strategic Movement or Strategic Sealift this turn (§7.2, rule 4).

7.4. Strategic Movement (2-space move)

A BG may move 2 land sectors in a single activation if both sectors it passes through/into are friendly-controlled and not Contested.

  • If the destination sector turns out to hold an enemy BG despite being expected-friendly, resolve the Engagement Check normally at the final sector only.

  • A BG that Strategic-Moves this turn cannot also Swap or Strategic Sealift this turn (§7.2, rule 4).

7.5. Strategic Sealift

A BG in a coastal land sector may, as its single activation, move directly to any other coastal land sector connected by an unbroken chain of sea sectors, provided:

  • No sea sector along the route is under enemy Sea Control (§11.4).

  • Naval Superiority penalties still apply. If any leg is contested and the enemy holds Naval Superiority there, the moving BG suffers the normal Interdiction penalty (−250 points per NTF of the enemy’s margin, §11.4).

  • This is for repositioning into empty or friendly coastal sectors. Landing on enemy-occupied territory is an Amphibious Invasion instead (§11.1).

  • A BG that uses Strategic Sealift this turn cannot also Swap or Strategic Movement this turn (§7.2, rule 4).

7.5.1. Tactical Sealift (house rule)

Strategic Sealift is deliberately RC-to-RC with no hop limit — genuine long-range power projection. That leaves a real gap: a small, RC-less outpost (Wake Island, Midway, and similar) that isn’t directly adjacent to a friendly BG has no valid way to receive one under any existing rule — it can’t be a Strategic Sealift destination (no RC), and it can’t be an Amphibious Invasion target either (that’s for taking enemy territory, not reinforcing your own).

Tactical Sealift is the short-range counterpart that fills that gap:

  • Range: 2 sea sectors, matching NTF Deployment Range’s own scale — not unlimited like Strategic Sealift.

  • Destination must be friendly-controlled or unoccupied and not Contested — same “empty or friendly” standard as Strategic Sealift.

  • No RC requirement on either end. This is the entire point — it’s for the small outposts Strategic Sealift structurally can’t reach.

  • Still blocked by enemy Sea Control along the route, the same as Strategic Sealift.

7.6. Movement mechanics

  • Spring/Summer/Fall: 1 land sector, or the enhanced options above (Strategic Movement, Swap, Strategic Sealift).

  • Winter weather sectors: max 1 sector, no strategic rail, no Strategic Movement through a weather sector, and −20% tactical budget if attacking.

  • Coordinated Attack. When a player’s movement into an enemy sector triggers an engagement with an opposing BG (not a spawned MIL BG), the attacking player may activate a second BG not previously activated in any adjacent sector to also enter the space and join the engagement. This counts as the second BG’s activation.

  • Mobile Reserve. When an attacking player’s movement into an enemy sector triggers an engagement, and the defending player has only 1 BG in the sector, the defending player may activate an adjacent ARM or MECH BG not previously activated to join the engagement. This counts as the second BG’s activation. The engagement automatically becomes a Meeting Engagement.

  • Rasputitsa (Mud Season). Spring and Fall turns, any sector in the Eastern Europe region: max 1 sector move, no Strategic Movement, and −10% tactical budget if attacking.

7.7. The Engagement Matrix

7.9. River Crossings

Ten sector connections cross a named river: the Rhine (Amsterdam–Ruhr), Elbe (Bavaria–Prague), Meuse (Paris–Brussels), Volga (Stalingrad–Rostov), Dnieper (Kiev–Rostov), Oder (Berlin–Warsaw), Yangtze (Shanghai–Nanking), Irrawaddy (Burma–India (Calcutta)), Volturno (Rome–Naples), and Danube (Vienna–Budapest).

When an attack crosses one of these connections into an uncontested sector (no friendly BG of the attacker’s already there), the river must be present in the WinSPWW2 engagement map. If the destination sector is already contested by a friendly BG (the attacker is reinforcing an existing fight rather than making a fresh crossing), no river is present in that engagement — the crossing already happened when the first BG got there.

8. Aftermath

Compare the loser’s final tactical score to the winner’s.

8.1. Outcomes

Loser’s score vs. winner’s Result
< 30% Decisive victory. Loser’s survivors are Shattered and retreat (§8.2–8.3). Winner: +5 XP. Loser: −5 XP.
30–70% Standard victory. Loser retreats (§8.2). No shatter. Winner: +3 XP.
> 70%, or a tied score Stalemate. Neither side retreats. Both BGs remain in the sector, which becomes/remains Contested. No experience change for either side (fatigue is still gained normally, §3.6). The standoff persists until one side voluntarily moves out (§7.2, opening it to Armor Exploitation if applicable), or a third BG enters and triggers a fresh engagement.

8.2. Retreat

The loser retreats all involved BGs to a single adjacent friendly or neutral sector.

8.3. Other outcomes

  • Annihilation. A defeated BG with no valid retreat sector is destroyed.

  • Armor Exploitation. A winning attacker who used an ARM BG may immediately push that BG into an adjacent enemy-occupied sector, triggering an Advance/Delay resolved this same turn (continues the victor’s activation streak under §7.2).

9.4. Contingent Bonus Precedence (Advanced)

If BGs from more than one country fight in the same engagement — pooled in a stack (§6), joined via Coordinated Attack or Mobile Reserve (§7.6), or reinforced by a Kampfgruppe (§12.1) — and more than one of those countries would contribute a national bonus (a National Token, §9.2, or a Minor Doctrine Rule, §13.1) to that fight, the owning player must declare one BG as that engagement’s Primary BG before Tactical Resolution.

Only the Primary BG’s country’s national bonuses apply. Every other involved BG’s national bonuses are suppressed for the fight — points, experience, standard Support Tokens, and all other rules still apply normally. The Primary BG choice is made fresh for each engagement.

Standard Support Tokens (§9.1) are never nation-locked and are unaffected. Elite Formation tokens (§9.3) are tied to each BG’s own half of the pool and apply regardless of which BG is Primary.

9.5. Doctrine Activation & Switching (Advanced)

  • Doctrine Slot. Each major nation has exactly one Doctrine slot, separate from its National Token slot (§9.2) — a nation may hold a Doctrine and a National Token at the same time. Only one of its two possible Doctrines (§9.2.1) may be active.

  • Activation. A Doctrine is declared during the Winter Logistics Phase and paid for in the themed tokens below. It takes effect the following Spring.

  • Lock-in. Once active, a Doctrine cannot be switched or dropped for 1 full year (4 strategic turns).

  • Switching. After lock-in expires, changing to the nation’s other Doctrine costs the new Doctrine’s full activation cost again, and that Winter’s entire Support Token collection is forfeited. The switch takes effect the following Spring. A nation can never hold both Doctrines simultaneously — activating the second automatically drops the first.

  • Costs outlive switching. A Doctrine’s structural cost runs its full stated course even after switching away, whether phrased as a fixed duration or as permanent/recurring. A Doctrine’s benefit ends immediately on switching. Exception: a cost phrased as a standing condition tied to the Doctrine itself (e.g. Panzerwaffe’s “if Germany’s fuel trace ever fails”) only applies while the Doctrine is active.

10. Winter Logistics Phase

End of the Winter turn only. In order:

  • Audit. Count secure, supplied RCs, and apply the ratio for the coming year (§2). (Soviet: evacuated industry is inactive for this step — see §12.4.)

  • Attrition. Each BG over the newly-set cap loses 20% of its points, applied in priority order: ARM → MECH → AIRBORNE → MARINE → INF.

  • Mobilization. If RC support exceeds BG count, build new BGs at full 2,500 points. Deploy each on any Owned RC sector (not Occupied — see §2.1) in the nation’s home territory that is not Damaged or Destroyed and currently traces supply to the capital (§4.1). The Home Capital itself always qualifies. Owner picks the type (respecting §3.2) and which eligible RC to deploy at.

  • Refit. Any Shattered BG that spent the Winter turn in a friendly RC sector returns to Ready.

  • Tokens. Collect 1 token per 2 RCs; choose type now.

  • Repair. Repair RCs damaged or destroyed by bombing or submarines.

(Soviet: evacuated RCs reactivate after all purchases are complete.)

11. Combined Arms & Strategic Warfare

11.1. Amphibious & Coordinated Airborne Invasions

An Amphibious Invasion is triggered by moving a Battle Group (BG) from a sea sector into an enemy-occupied land sector. It represents a highly complex, coordinated landing against a hostile shoreline.

11.1.1 Staging and Operational Limits

  • Origin. The invading BG must stage from a friendly-controlled land or sea sector. An invasion cannot be launched out of contested or enemy-controlled territory.

  • Range. The target enemy-occupied land sector must be within 2 sea sectors of the staging sector, counting the unbroken sea-sector chain between them. Longer-range landings are prohibited.

  • Intelligence Status. A BG staged in a sea sector loaded for an invasion is always exposed at a minimum of Tier 2 (Partial Info) — the attacker cannot hide that an invasion fleet is active, only its exact composition.

11.1.2 Coordinated Airborne Vanguard Option

An amphibious landing can be executed as a Joint-Force Operation by coordinating with an Airborne Invasion (§11.2) launched into the exact same target sector during the same strategic turn.

  • Execution Order. The Phase 0 Airborne Drop is resolved first.

  • Airborne Success. If the Airborne BG wins, an Airborne Lodgement is established. The subsequent Beach Assault (Phase 1) receives: (1) Artillery Cutoff — the defender cannot assign ART tokens; (2) Blocked Reserves — the defender cannot activate a Mobile Reserve this turn; (3) Fortifications stay intact — the defender keeps their full Assault/Defend fortifications budget (+750 points).

  • Airborne Failure. If the Airborne BG loses, it is Annihilated. The lodgement fails, the beach assault is cancelled, and no further BGs may enter the sector.

11.1.3 Resolution Phases

If no Airborne Vanguard is used, or the Vanguard succeeds, the invasion resolves as two sequential tactical battles:

  • Phase 1: The Beach Assault — Assault/Defend parameters. Defeat: the invading BGs retreat to their staging sea sectors and suffer an extra 50% points loss; the invasion ends. Victory: proceed to Phase 2.

  • Phase 2: Inland Breakout — Advance/Delay against the same defender’s survivors. If an Airborne Vanguard was used, the Airborne BG does not participate (it’s holding the rear). Defeat: the conventional forces bounce back to staging; an un-relieved Airborne BG is left surrounded and permanently destroyed at the end of the movement phase. Stalemate: both sides remain, sector becomes/remains Contested; Follow-On Exploitation is blocked, no out-of-sequence reinforcements may land, and relieving these forces requires a Strategic Swap Move (§7.3) — an Airborne BG present survives but stays stuck in the pocket. Victory: the defender retreats, the sector is secured, and an active Amphibious Lodgement is established — the sector’s stacking limit is temporarily raised to 4 BGs until the end of the current strategic turn.

11.1.4 Amphibious Follow-On Exploitation

On a Decisive or Standard victory in Phase 2, the attacker may immediately exploit with fresh follow-on waves: up to 2× BGs that haven’t activated yet this turn, are in a connected coastal or secure sea staging sector, and aren’t subject to naval interdiction on their route (no enemy Naval Superiority or Sea Control), may execute an out-of-sequence Strategic Sealift directly into the secured sector. This counts as each BG’s activation for the turn, and fills the temporary 4-BG cap.

11.1.5 Lodgement Contraction

If Follow-On Exploitation was used to stack up to 4 BGs, the player must reduce the sector back to the standard 2-BG limit during the very next strategic turn’s movement phase, by moving the excess out to adjacent friendly or empty sectors. Failing to do so by the end of that turn’s movement phase subjects the excess BGs to immediate, permanent over-stacking attrition — 20% of their points lost per turn.

Pacific island hops. A small number of Pacific sectors are shown as directly connected for movement despite being separated by open ocean (e.g. New Guinea–Solomon Islands, Philippines–Mindanao–Dutch East Indies, Marshall–Caroline–Gilbert Islands, Hawaii to the US West Coast or Mexico) — this is shorthand for sea movement between those land spaces without a separately-drawn sea sector, not a distinct category of link. Moving into an enemy-occupied sector across one of these always resolves under this section, never a plain Engagement Check. This does not apply to genuinely contiguous mainland (e.g. Australia’s Sydney–Darwin–Perth) or to movement between the Japanese Home Islands, which follow their own rules (§12.2).

Pacific Typhoon Season. Fall turns, any Amphibious Invasion staged from or transiting a Pacific-region sea sector suffers Interdiction (−250 points, §11.4) as if contested by 1 enemy NTF, even if no enemy NTF is present.

Western Europe: Post-Lodgement Stacking. A successful Amphibious Lodgement (§11.1.3) reaches the full 4-BG stacking cap for free, automatically, for the strategic turn of the victory only — this is unconditional. Sustaining elevated capacity past that turn requires one of two completely independent paths, described below (§11.1.6, §11.1.7). Neither depends on the other.

11.1.6 Lodgement Rotation. Reflecting the Western Allies’ superior sea-lift and logistics capacity, the Allied player may spend 1× AMPH token every turn — no RC capture required — to maintain Lodgement Rotation at a secured invasion sector. While active and paid up for the current turn, that sector and every sector directly adjacent to it together support up to 6 BGs total — a single shared pool across the whole zone, not 6 in each sector individually. As with a Home Capital (§6), only 2 BGs ever fight at a time in a given engagement (the player’s choice when resolving it) — fresh Battle Groups rotate in while battle-worn ones rest, rather than everyone fighting simultaneously. The AMPH token must be spent again on every subsequent turn to keep it running; skipping a turn’s payment immediately reverts the cap to 2 (or to whatever a nearby RC provides, per §11.1.7) and normal Contraction rules (§11.1.5) apply to any excess.

11.1.7 Deep-Water Port Capture. Separately, and requiring no AMPH token, the Allied player may instead physically capture and secure an adjacent land sector containing a Resource Centre. This is not a special lodgement mechanic — the captured RC simply operates normally, exactly like any other RC on the map (economy, mobilization eligibility, etc.), with no stacking bonus of its own. It’s a legitimate long-term alternative to paying for Rotation, just not one that grants extra stacking capacity by itself.

11.2. Airborne Invasion

Triggered when an Airborne BG using an ABN token moves into an enemy-occupied sector. Range: 1 or 2 sectors.

Resolved as a single engagement — Airborne Insertion, Meeting Engagement parameters, attacker may purchase airborne units only. Loss → the BG is destroyed. Win → the sector is secured (an Airborne Lodgement is established there).

This is the entire Airborne Invasion regardless of what happens next — the same one engagement, the same two outcomes, whether it’s launched standalone, paired with an Amphibious Invasion as a Vanguard (§11.1.2, where a win additionally unlocks that operation’s Lodgement Benefits going into its own separate Beach Assault), or followed up afterward by an ordinary Coordinated Attack (§7.6) from other forces. The Airborne engagement itself never gains or loses a second phase depending on context — anything that follows is a separate action, not an extension of this one.

11.3. Strategic Bombing (USA/UK)

Declared before Movement. Allies assign STRAT tokens to Axis RCs; Axis may assign Fighter tokens to defend; Allies may assign Fighter tokens to escort.

Basing requirements: - German RCs → Allies must hold Southern England - Italian RCs → Allies must hold Tunisia - Japanese RCs → Allies must hold Formosa or the Marianas

Resolution. Count tokens. Each Allied Fighter cancels one Axis Fighter. Each surviving Axis Fighter cancels one STRAT token. If ≥1 bomber survives, the strike succeeds.

Effects (same regardless of surviving bomber count): - 1st hit → Damaged: RC generates no Support Token next Winter. - 2nd hit → Destroyed: RC generates no Support Token and no BG next Winter.

Repair (Winter Logistics): Damaged → Active costs 1 token of any type. Destroyed → Active costs 2 tokens (one may be spent to step it down to Damaged).

11.5. Submarine Warfare

Spend a Submarine token during Movement on one of: - Interdiction — as Naval Superiority above. - Convoy Hunting — in an ocean sector with no enemy NTF and adjacent to an enemy RC, that RC becomes Damaged. No effect if already Damaged. - Convoy Raiding — if the USA assigns a Lend-Lease token (§9.2) to a non-US Allied BG this turn, and the delivery route (Arctic to USSR, Atlantic to UK) passes through a sea sector where Germany holds Naval Superiority or Sea Control (§11.4), Germany may spend a Submarine token to sink the convoy, cancelling the Lend-Lease token’s effect entirely.

11.6. Naval Task Force (NTF) Deployment Range

To simulate the logistical drag of blue-water operations and force a realistic island-hopping campaign, all NTFs deployed during Movement must trace a viable line of communication back to an active RC controlled by that same nation — not merely the same faction. A UK NTF traces to a UK-controlled RC specifically; it cannot draw range from a USA, USSR, or China RC just because they’re all Allied. Each nation’s fleet earns its own reach.

  • Base Range. An NTF may only deploy to a sea sector within 2 sea sectors of a land sector controlled by that same nation and containing an active RC (or a Forward Staging Base or Mobile Staging Anchorage, both below), tracing the shortest contiguous chain of sea sectors from the target back to that anchor.

  • USN Mobile Staging Anchorages. There is no flat USN range bonus. Instead, any sea sector holding 3 or more USN NTFs acts as a virtual active RC for range-tracing purposes for other USN NTFs — range projection has to be physically concentrated and earned, not assumed. A Mobile Staging Anchorage also counts as a valid friendly staging point for an Amphibious Invasion (§11.1) even without nearby coastal territory, and as valid carrier-based air support satisfying Japan’s Strategic Bombing basing requirement (§11.3) if within 2 sea sectors of the target RC.

  • Forward Staging Bases (Seabees). During any Alternating Movement phase, if a US Amphibious or Marine BG occupies a coastal land or island sector with an RC value of 0, the US may spend 1× ENG or AMPH token to instantly upgrade that sector into a Forward Staging Base: it generates no economic points and supports no extra BGs, but acts as an active RC solely for tracing NTF deployment range.

  • The Island-Hopping Supply Link. When tracing the path from a target sea sector back to an active RC (or Forward Staging Base / Mobile Staging Anchorage), the path may cross at most one (1) land sector. An NTF cannot bridge multiple island-to-island or island-to-mainland connections (e.g. New Guinea to the Solomons) to trace supply — players must capture intermediate islands and build Forward Staging Bases to step the fleet’s reach forward.

  • Gibraltar (special mixed land/sea sector). Gibraltar is the sole sea-to-sea link between the Atlantic and the Western Mediterranean. As long as it is held by the standing UK garrison — a permanent fortress presence, not a Battle Group, never at risk from an engagement roll — the strait is closed to Axis navy entirely: no Axis NTF may deploy there, and no Axis NTF range tracing or amphibious route may pass through it. This is a hard block, not Interdiction. Held by the UK garrison from scenario start.

  • Suez. The sole sea-to-sea link between the Eastern Mediterranean and the Red Sea, functioning as an ordinary chokepoint sea sector — whoever holds Naval Superiority or Sea Control there (§11.4) gates the route in the normal way.

  • UK Home Waters Dominance. As long as the UK holds its home territory (London or Midlands), it has a standing +1 Naval Superiority margin in the North Sea and English Channel, representing the Home Fleet’s default presence. This is always on — the UK does not need to assign an NTF there to benefit from it, though assigned NTFs still add to the total normally.

11.7. Desert Outflanking (North Africa / Middle East)

An INF or MIL BG defending in a North Africa or Middle East sector against an attacking ARM or MECH BG cannot simply hold on a Stalemate result: the defender is forced to retreat 1 land sector, with no extra retreat penalties or XP losses.

Exceptions (the defender holds normally):

  • The sector is a defined bottleneck (El Alamein).
  • The sector’s major terrain (§3.1.1) is Mountain.
  • The sector is a coastal RC sector under the defender’s own control (a fortified port).
  • An ANZAC Battle Group is present among the defenders — see the updated Diggers rule (§13.1): their reputation for holding ground in the desert (Tobruk, El Alamein) extends to exempting them from Forced Retrograde entirely, on top of the existing Fatigue immunity.

13.3. Neutrality Violation (house rule)

Most of the map’s 18 Neutral countries — Afghanistan, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Cuba, Ireland, Mexico, Mongolia, Peru, Saudi Arabia, Switzerland, Tibet, Venezuela, and the ones already covered by §13’s table above — have no combat garrison worth mentioning. Nothing in the base rules stops a player from simply walking in and taking them for free, which doesn’t match the history and isn’t interesting play. This house rule doesn’t block it — it makes it cost something.

  • Trigger. Moving a Battle Group into any Neutral-owned sector prompts a confirmation before the move commits. Declining cancels the move entirely; nothing is lost by backing out.

  • Effect, on confirmation. The invading player picks which nation of the opposing faction receives the consequence (there’s no single obvious “the Allies” recipient in a multi-nation alliance, so the invader names one explicitly): that nation gets a standing +0.5 RC added to its Cap Audit total, and 2 free Support Tokens of the invader’s-opponent’s choice, both permanent and immediate.

  • Stacking. The +0.5 RC bonus is cumulative per nation — two separate violations awarded to the same nation total +1 RC, three total +1.5, and so on. There’s no cap.

  • This applies regardless of whether the target sector is already covered by a specific §13 activation trigger — the two systems aren’t mutually exclusive. Invading Iraq, for instance, still triggers its own automatic Summer 1941 activation independently of this house rule.

v6 → v7 (consistency audit)

  • Fixed a duplicate section number. Two different sections were both numbered §3.4 (Cavalry and Experience). Experience is now §3.5, Fatigue §3.6, Recovery §3.7 — every cross-reference to the old numbers throughout the document has been updated to match.
  • Added §3.1.1 Terrain Types. A real, previously undocumented mechanic — sectors have a major terrain type (8 categories) shown in their panel, which is reference information for choosing among the Combat Resolver’s five tactical terrain categories (Open/Steppe, Urban, Mountain/Jungle, Amphibious Landing, Arctic/Tundra) that actually multiply BG effective points by type. Two existing rules (Alpini Doctrine, Desert Outflanking) referenced “major terrain” and cited the wrong section (§3.1, the Battle Group types table) — both now correctly point to §3.1.1.
  • Added §6.1 Small Island Outposts. A house rule that was fully implemented and enforced in the campaign tool but had no corresponding rulebook entry at all: Wake Island, Midway, Fiji, Samoa, Gilbert Islands, and Marshall Islands are hard-capped at 1 BG per faction, overriding even an adjacent Lodgement Rotation pool.
  • Terminology cleanup. §12.6 and §13.1 used “Occupy_RC” / “Owner_RC,” which don’t match the campaign tool’s actual field names. Changed to plain “Owner” / “Occupier” throughout.
  • Clarified NTF Deployment Range (§11.6) is nation-scoped, not faction-scoped. The previous wording (“friendly Resource Centre”) was ambiguous between “same nation” and “same faction” — this ambiguity previously caused a real bug where a nation’s NTF range was incorrectly computed from its whole faction’s combined RC network rather than its own. The rule text now says so explicitly.
  • Tagged all six Advanced-module sections inline (§2.1, §9.2.1, §9.4, §9.5, §13.1, §13.2) with (Advanced) next to their headers, so the Core/Advanced split declared in the introduction is usable while reading through the document, not just stated once at the top and then forgotten.

v7 → v8

  • Italian Armistice trigger simplified (§12.6). Was Sicily plus any 2 of {Naples, Rome, Milan}; now Sicily plus just 1 of those 3. The campaign tool now also prompts the Allied player directly — not the Axis player — once the condition is met, since the Allies are the ones taking the action that triggers it.

v8 → v9

  • §13.1 Vichy trigger renamed and fixed. “Compiègne Armistice” is now “Case Anton,” matching the actual historical operation (Germany’s occupation of Vichy France following Operation Torch — Compiègne refers to the 1940 armistice that created Vichy France, a different event entirely).
  • Corrected the trigger sectors. The old text named “(Vichy, Tunis)” as the North African trigger — but the sector literally named “Vichy” is in metropolitan France, not North Africa, and Tunis was already German-occupied, not Vichy-controlled. Neither was a sector an Allied invasion could actually target under the rule’s own intent. Now correctly reads Morocco or Algiers — the two sectors that are genuinely both North African and still under direct Vichy control.
  • Narrowed what Case Anton actually annexes. Previously “all remaining Vichy sectors” at once — meaning a single invasion of French North Africa could instantly flip the entire Vichy colonial empire to Germany, which both wasn’t historically accurate (the real Case Anton took metropolitan France only; the colonies kept fracturing on their own for years after) and made the ongoing Free France vs. Vichy contest largely pointless the moment it fired. Case Anton now annexes only Vichy’s 2 metropolitan sectors (Vichy, Marseille); colonial territory stays a live contest for Free France to Rally or the Allies to capture.
  • §13.2’s “full collaboration” endgame (3 Vichy Legitimacy) now does the job the old version of Case Anton used to do — it’s the point where whatever colonial remnant is left finally gets absorbed too. Added a second path to reach it (ordinary Axis combat capture of a Vichy sector, not just Case Anton), since Case Anton alone can now only ever contribute +2 toward the 3-point threshold.

v9 → v10

  • Support Tokens no longer carry forward indefinitely (§2, house rule). Unspent tokens now clear at the end of the year, right before that Winter’s fresh collection replaces them — spend down before Winter Logistics runs, or lose whatever’s left. The campaign tool shows exactly what was cleared, per nation, before the new collection begins.
  • Manual token adjustment restored, in a more controlled form. Regular Support Tokens gained a real collection mechanic recently (a guided “pick a type for each token you earned” flow instead of freely editing numbers) — but sometimes a manual correction is still genuinely needed. Added dedicated Buy (+1) / Spend (−1) controls per nation and type, and the tool now flags a nation’s STRAT holding directly in the inventory table if it exceeds that nation’s §9.1 build cap for the current year — the only Support Token type with an actual rules-defined numeric limit.
